# Where-is-it AI — Discovery Record

## A. Interview Protocol

**Target (who, and why):** University students who, within the past month, have actually searched for an item in their living space, such as a studio apartment or dormitory. They are selected so that, through a recent and specific experience, we can confirm where they started looking, the order of their search, the time it took, where the item was found, and the inconveniences they experienced along the way.

**What we want to find out (hypothesis):** The time it takes to find an item comes less from situations where the person has no idea where the item is, and more from repeatedly checking the places where they usually keep things while under time pressure, such as right before going out. In addition, even though existing tracking tools like Find My and Apple Tag can show an item's approximate location, they will have limitations when it comes to finding the exact spot where the item is actually placed indoors.

**5 Questions**

1. Tell me about the most recent time you had difficulty finding something. When and how did you realize that you could not find the item? (Q3, Q4) — A specific past event and its trigger
2. Where did you look first, and why did you check that place first? Where did you search after that, and in what order? (Q5, Q6) — The actual search order and the reasoning behind it
3. Have you ever used an app, tracking device, or another person's help to find an item? If yes, did it actually help? (Q7) — Currently hired alternatives and their limitations
4. How long did it take, and what was the most inconvenient part of the search? (Q8) — Actual cost (time, emotion)
5. Where did you finally find the item, and what made you check that place? (Q9) — Final action, and actual location vs. expected location

**Role of the Remaining Questions**

| Question | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Q1. Do you sometimes have trouble remembering where you put your things? | Participant selection (screening) | A yes/no question, so all 10 participants answered "yes." Used only for screening, not as information |
| Q2. What kinds of items do you usually have trouble finding? | Identifying item categories | "Usually" is a generalizing question that relies on memory. Cross-checked against the specific event in Q3 |
| Q7 follow-up. Depending on the answer, ask about the good points and shortcomings of the tools or apps they currently use | In-depth probing of the limits of current alternatives | The "it only shows that it's nearby" finding in Logs 6 and 9 came from here |
| Q10. What information or help would have helped you find the item faster at that time? | Exploring the information needed | Because "at that time" ties it to a past event, it is better than a purely hypothetical question, but answers easily drift toward solution ideas |

**Leading Questions We Must Be Especially Careful About**

❌ "Would you use an app that tells you where things are?" — Future, hypothetical, leading. "Yes, sounds good" is not information.

✅ "Do you remember when and where you last used that item?" — Actual past behavior.

> ⚠️ Among the answers to Q10, solution ideas (the CCTV in Log 5, the Find Me app in Log 8) are marked in the log as **opinions** only and are not used as evidence. On the other hand, answers that reveal "what they didn't know at that time that made it take so long" (Log 10's "checking the same places again") are treated as descriptions of past behavior and used as evidence.

**Derived Pains (cumulative)**

- **Searching under time pressure right before going out** [Problem] — Noticing the item is missing right before class, work, or paying; spending 5–15 minutes, with stress and risk of being late (Logs 1, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10)
- **Repeatedly checking the usual place first** [Workaround] — All 10 participants look at "the place they usually keep it" first, then expand to its surroundings → bed, desk, bag. This leads to checking the same places again (Logs 1–10, especially 7, 10)
- **Relying on memory of the last-used place** [Workaround] — In many of the cases where the item was found, it was resolved by recalling "the place where it was last used" (Logs 1, 6, 10)
- **Asking other people** [Workaround] (Logs 4, 5, 10)
- **Existing trackers only go as far as "nearby"** [Failure mode] — They show only an approximate location, not the exact location within the room. They only attach to specific devices such as Apple's (Logs 1, 6, 9)
- **Items that are small, thin, or hidden under other items** [Constraint] — Hair ties, cards, earbuds, documents, glasses, etc. Hidden under clothes, pillows, or inside bags (Logs 1, 2, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10)
- **Mismatch between expected and actual location** [Failure mode] — Stress increases when the item isn't "where it should be," and it turns up in unexpected places (Logs 2, 4, 9)
- **The usual place becomes vague for infrequently used items** [Constraint] — Infrequently used items like pills take several days (Logs 2, 5)

**Negative Signals (logs that challenge the hypothesis)**

- Logs 3, 7, 9: Participants look for the solution in their own habits — "I need to be more careful and organized." The inertia of self-blame may be stronger than demand for a tool.
- Log 5: When it's not urgent, the participant isn't very bothered even when it takes 3 days → the pain may be limited to **urgent moments**.
- Logs 3, 7, 8: Participants use no apps or devices at all → there is a user group with no habit of using tracking tools in the first place.

## C. Workflow Observation Record

Observation 1 — Participant: Participant 6 (respondent of Log 6) / Date/Time: 9/12 14:00 / Method: Reenactment via screen sharing (about 20 minutes)

| Observation Item | What Was Observed | Spec Candidate (what it translates into) |
|---|---|---|
| Steps and tools | ① Needs AirPods to go to class → ② Checks the table where they are usually kept → ③ Not there, so checks the bag → ④ Since it's an Apple accessory, uses Find My Device → ⑤ Confirms on the map that they are nearby → ⑥ Checks the bed where they were previously used → ⑦ Finds the AirPods in a bag under the pillow | Need to connect usual storage place → surrounding places → location tracking tool → last-used place into a single search flow |
| Handoff | After not finding them on the table at ②, moves to the bag at ③. Then at ④, switches from physical search to the location tracking app. Because the app can't show the exact location, switches back to physical search at ⑥ | Need a feature that can connect location tracking information with physical search of the space |
| Workaround | Confirmed via Find My Device that the AirPods were nearby but couldn't learn the exact location, so rechecked the bed where the AirPods had previously been used | Need information that narrows down the last-used place or expected location, not just simple "nearby" location information |
| Hesitation | Used Find My Device, but because they already knew the item was nearby, gained no additional exact location information. Afterwards, had to search the actual places again | Provide additional location clues during the search process after "it's nearby" |

**Difference between statement and observation:** In Log 6, the participant said Find My Device "only tells you that it's nearby." In the reenactment, they went through a total of 4 search steps: table → bag → Find My Device → under the pillow. They said the time cost was about 10 minutes in total. They did not repeatedly search the same place, but the very fact that they had to switch between physical searching and tool use across 4 steps is confirmed as the cost of the item-finding Job. In particular, since they had to search the physical space again even after using Find My Device, the current tool functions as one step in the search process rather than ending the search.

## D. Core Job Story

**Core Job Story**

> When I can't see an item in its usual place at the moment I need to go out or use it, I want to quickly narrow down its possible locations and find it, so that I can reduce the time spent repeatedly checking the same places or searching the whole room. (Logs 1, 3, 6, 8, 9, 10)

| Element | Content | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Situation (trigger) | Discovering the item is not in its usual place at a moment when it's needed right away, such as class, work, or paying | Logs 1, 3, 6, 8, 10 |
| Functional Job | Quickly finding the exact location of an item that couldn't be found in its usual place | Logs 6, 9, 10 |
| Emotional Job | Reducing the stress of "it's not where it should be," the stress of not remembering where it was put, and the anxiety of possibly being late | Logs 4, 9, 10 |
| Social Job | Wanting to avoid being late or having to keep asking other people for help | Logs 4, 5, 9, 10 |
| Currently hired alternatives | Check usual place → search surroundings → search likely places such as bag/bed/desk → recall last-used place → ask other people → use tracking app | Logs 1–10, Observation 1 |

**The Four Forces of Switching**

| Force | Content | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Push | 5–15 minutes spent repeatedly checking the same places, risk of being late. Trackers only say "nearby," so in the end they search by hand | Logs 6, 9, 10 |
| Pull | A solution that reduces search time by telling the exact location of the item or the location where it was last used/placed | (Hypothesis) |
| Anxiety | Privacy concerns about placing a camera in one's room. Anxiety about whether a new finder can always tell the item's location accurately. In particular, worry about camera blind spots, failing to recognize items that are too small or hidden, or misclassifying items. Also anxiety that, when there are several items of the same kind, the AI may fail to distinguish the specific lost item from others and point to the wrong location (e.g., misrecognizing a lighter as a lipstick, or, when there are two wallets, seeing the other wallet instead of the lost one and saying "the wallet is on the table.") | (Hypothesis) |
| Habit | "I just need to be more careful and organized" — the inertia of solving it through one's own habits instead of a tool. Many don't use any tools at all | Logs 3, 7, 8, 9 |

**Rewriting in ontology terms:** Request context (User, target Item, urgency) → Observation record (the Location where the Item was last detected, time, whether it was hidden) → Outcome (search ends with exact location guidance)
